## Three-Step Drop

Key: `workout:three-step-drop` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Move back from the line and finish in a balanced throwing position.

**Setup:** Stand facing your pretend line of scrimmage with soft knees and your feet comfortably apart.

**Equipment:** Clear, level space; ball optional

1. **Choose your line.** Pick a safe spot in front of you to represent the line. Look back and check that your practice lane is clear.
2. **Walk the pattern.** In this draft right-handed example, step back with your right foot, cross your left foot behind, then finish with your right foot underneath you. Left-handed passers mirror the feet. Use your coach's pattern if different.
3. **Finish ready.** Stop your backward travel. Keep your head level, knees soft and feet apart enough to balance.
4. **Check and return.** Pause with your eyes on a safe target. Walk forward to your starting spot before trying again.

**Coaching cue:** The last step gets you ready to throw. It should not leave you falling backward.

**Common errors:** Taking a huge final step and leaning back. Looking down at every step instead of checking your finish.

**Safety:** Start at walking speed without a throw. Have a parent or coach check the space for younger athletes. Confirm the foot sequence with Coach Tae before adding speed; stop for pain, dizziness or loss of balance.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (4 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Five-Step Drop

Key: `workout:five-step-drop` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Practice a longer drop without letting the last steps carry you off balance.

**Setup:** Face your pretend line with a clear lane behind you and the ball held comfortably near your chest.

**Equipment:** A clear backward lane; ball optional

1. **Check the lane.** Walk the full practice lane first. Leave room to stop before any fence, wall or person.
2. **Count your steps.** Walk your coach-taught five-step pattern. A draft right-handed example is right-left-right-left-right moving away from the line; a left-handed passer mirrors it. This count is not universal drop timing.
3. **Gather the last steps.** Let the final steps bring your feet underneath you. Do not keep reaching farther backward.
4. **Hold your platform.** Finish with your eyes downfield and pause without wobbling. Return to the start by walking.

**Coaching cue:** A longer drop still needs a quiet finish. Count slowly before you try to move faster.

**Common errors:** Speeding up the count until the steps become a run. Landing on a narrow base with no room to reset.

**Safety:** Use a longer clear lane than for a three-step drop. Begin without throwing. Under-center and shotgun footwork differ; confirm this pattern before using it in a play. Stop if anything hurts.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (5 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## 3-Step Drop + Shuffle

Key: `workout:three-step-drop-shuffle` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Get your feet ready to throw quickly and stay balanced.

**Setup:** Stand in your quarterback stance, facing a safe imaginary target. Keep the lane behind and ahead of you clear.

**Equipment:** No equipment; ball optional

1. **Get ready.** Bend your knees slightly and hold the ball securely, or bring your hands together if you have no ball.
2. **Take your drop.** Use your coach-taught three-step drop. For this draft right-handed walkthrough: right foot back, left crosses behind, right finishes underneath you. Left-handed passers mirror it. Walk first.
3. **Get balanced.** Stop your backward travel with your feet underneath you and your eyes downfield.
4. **Shuffle up.** Make one short, controlled move forward toward the line. Move the leading foot first and let the other follow, without bringing your feet together.
5. **Reset and repeat.** Pause in your ready position. Walk to the starting spot. Add a gentle throw only when the pattern is comfortable and a safe partner is ready.

**Coaching cue:** Do not rush your feet. Finish the shuffle balanced, so you can choose when to throw.

**Common errors:** Shuffling before the drop has stopped. Clicking the feet together or crossing them during the forward shuffle.

**Safety:** Try the movement without throwing first. Use level ground and clear space; a parent or coach should help younger athletes choose the area. The drop sequence is a draft system example. Coach Tae must approve footwork and dose. Stop for pain or discomfort.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (5 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Five-Step Drop + Shuffle

Key: `workout:five-step-drop-shuffle` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Connect a longer drop to a small forward reset without rushing the transition.

**Setup:** Face an imaginary target with enough room behind you for your coach-taught five-step drop.

**Equipment:** Clear practice lane; ball optional

1. **Walk the five.** Count your five-step pattern at a walking pace. In the draft right-handed example the feet go right-left-right-left-right; mirror for a left-handed example and confirm your system's pattern.
2. **Stop the drop.** Gather your feet into a comfortable base before starting the next movement.
3. **Move forward once.** Take one small forward shuffle with the leading foot followed by the other. Keep space between your feet.
4. **Freeze and check.** Hold a ready throwing position. If you are still drifting, slow the next attempt down. Walk back to reset.

**Coaching cue:** Treat the drop and shuffle as two connected actions, not one backward-and-forward sprint.

**Common errors:** Adding several shuffles when the drill calls for one. Leaning forward to make up for feet that are still moving backward.

**Safety:** Rehearse without a throw and check both ends of the lane. Do not use the draft step count as a universal play progression. Stop if pain or balance problems appear.

## Three-Cone Footwork

Key: `workout:three-cone-footwork` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Change direction around simple markers while keeping your feet under control.

**Setup:** Place one marker at a start spot and one to either side, with enough space for a comfortable shuffle. Do not use hard or sharp objects.

**Equipment:** Three flat markers, or safe spots on the ground

1. **Set your base.** Start behind the middle marker with soft knees and your eyes up.
2. **Travel left.** Shuffle toward the left marker. Lead with the left foot and let the right follow without crossing.
3. **Change direction.** Stop before the marker, then shuffle back through the middle toward the right marker.
4. **Finish in the middle.** Return to the middle and pause in a ready throwing position. Reset the markers if the steps feel cramped.

**Coaching cue:** Control the stop at each marker. Moving a little slower is better than sliding past your spot.

**Common errors:** Stepping on a marker instead of stopping beside it. Crossing feet while trying to cover too much space.

**Safety:** Use flat, visible markers on non-slip ground and keep the lane clear. Choose comfortable spacing rather than a fixed distance. Stop for pain or an unstable footing.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (5 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Pocket Ladder

Key: `workout:pocket-ladder` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Practice short, controlled steps through a lane without looking down the whole time.

**Setup:** Make a short ladder with flat marks spaced for your comfortable steps. Walk it once to check that nothing catches your shoes.

**Equipment:** Flat tape marks or a flat agility ladder; optional ball

1. **Enter slowly.** Face forward in a comfortable base. Step into the first space with your eyes briefly checking the lane.
2. **Keep small steps.** Move forward one space at a time. Let the second foot follow without stamping or jumping.
3. **Look ahead.** As the path becomes familiar, lift your eyes toward a safe target instead of staring at your feet.
4. **Exit ready.** Step clear of the last mark and stop balanced. Walk around the outside to return.

**Coaching cue:** The ladder is a guide for control. It does not set the foot rhythm for every pass play.

**Common errors:** Racing the marks and losing a comfortable base. Hopping both feet through every space.

**Safety:** Use a flat ladder, never raised obstacles. Skip the equipment if it creates a trip risk. Do not throw while stepping over the ladder. Stop if you trip or feel pain.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (4 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Reset Your Feet

Key: `workout:reset-your-feet` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Turn a small movement into a stable new throwing position.

**Setup:** Stand comfortably with one pretend target straight ahead and another slightly to one side.

**Equipment:** Two safe imaginary targets; no equipment

1. **Face the first target.** Set your feet comfortably and hold your hands near the ball position.
2. **Find the second.** Move your eyes to the other target, then move your feet to support that direction.
3. **Gather, do not twist.** Use small steps to turn your base. Avoid twisting on a foot stuck firmly in the ground.
4. **Check the finish.** Pause and check that you can stand without leaning. Switch back to the first target with another small reset.

**Coaching cue:** Move your base toward the new target; do not ask only your arm to make the change.

**Common errors:** Turning the shoulders while the feet stay pointed elsewhere. Taking a large jump to reset.

**Safety:** Start without a ball or throw. Use gentle angles and clear space. Do not force a knee or ankle to twist. Stop for pain.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (3 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Quick-Game Footwork

Key: `workout:quick-game-footwork` · Footwork

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Get ready for a short timing concept without hurrying the throw.

**Setup:** Choose the footwork your coach uses for one short-pass concept. If it is not known, use a simple catch-and-set walkthrough without throwing.

**Equipment:** Ball optional; safe short target if throwing

1. **Name the setup.** Decide whether you are rehearsing an under-center or shotgun start. Do not swap their step counts automatically.
2. **Catch or begin.** For the simple walkthrough, bring your hands to the ball position as if receiving a snap.
3. **Set your base.** Make the small adjustment needed to finish balanced toward your imaginary target.
4. **See before you act.** Look at the target and pause. A quick concept still needs a decision; do not throw only because your feet stopped.

**Coaching cue:** Quick means prepared. Your system's route timing decides when the ball comes out.

**Common errors:** Using the same number of steps for every formation and short pass. Throwing before checking whether the target is ready or available.

**Safety:** Confirm the concept and footwork with Coach Tae before adding speed. Only throw to a prepared partner in a clear area; stop for pain or fatigue that changes your motion.

## Step Up in the Pocket

Key: `workout:step-up-pocket` · Pocket Movement

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Move a little forward when space is open, then get ready to throw again.

**Setup:** Stand behind an imaginary line in a balanced throwing position. Choose an open spot a small step ahead.

**Equipment:** Clear floor or grass; optional flat start marker

1. **Check forward space.** Look at the spot ahead. In a game, interior pressure can make stepping up unsafe; this drill rehearses the clear-space case only.
2. **Make a short move.** Move the forward foot toward that space and let the other follow. Stay behind your imaginary line of scrimmage.
3. **Reset your base.** Finish with soft knees and your feet apart, without standing tall or lunging.
4. **Find the target again.** Bring your eyes to the target and pause. Walk back to the starting position.

**Coaching cue:** Space ahead is the reason to step up. Forward movement is not automatically the right answer.

**Common errors:** Stepping toward an imaginary interior rusher. Taking such a long step that a throw would start from a lunge.

**Safety:** No live pass rusher or contact is needed. Keep the spot ahead empty. Begin without throwing and stop for pain or loss of balance.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (4 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Escape and Reset

Key: `workout:escape-reset` · Pocket Movement

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Leave an imagined pressure area and regain control in clear space.

**Setup:** Stand in a ready position with an imaginary pressure spot in front and a clear exit to one side.

**Equipment:** A wide clear lane; optional flat marker

1. **Choose the safe exit.** Point out an open lane to the side. Do not practice blindly spinning toward an unknown space.
2. **Leave under control.** Turn and move into the open lane at an easy pace, keeping the ball secure if you have one.
3. **Slow and gather.** Use small steps to stop outside the imagined pocket. Give yourself room to brake.
4. **Reset to throw.** Turn your feet and body toward a safe target, pause balanced, then walk to the start. This version ends with a set platform.

**Coaching cue:** Escaping pressure and being ready to pass are different tasks. Finish both before adding a throw.

**Common errors:** Running directly into the imagined pressure spot. Trying to force a throw before slowing down.

**Safety:** Use no live defender or obstacles. Start at walking speed and do not throw across people. Passing and QB-running legality depend on the selected tackle, flag or event rules. Stop for pain.

## QB Stance and Ball Position

Key: `workout:qb-stance-ball-position` · Throwing Mechanics

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Find a comfortable starting position that lets you move and prepare to throw.

**Setup:** Stand on level ground with your feet comfortably apart and a slight bend in your knees.

**Equipment:** Football optional

1. **Find your base.** Shift gently until your weight feels supported by both feet. Avoid locking your knees.
2. **Hold securely.** If you have a ball, hold it comfortably near your upper body with your non-throwing hand supporting it. Exact carriage varies by coach and situation.
3. **Relax the upper body.** Keep the grip secure without squeezing hard or lifting your shoulders toward your ears.
4. **Test the position.** Take a small step in either direction, then return. Adjust if your stance feels too wide or too narrow to move.

**Coaching cue:** Your ready position should help you move. It should not feel like a pose you are stuck holding.

**Common errors:** Holding the ball far away from the body for no reason. Stiff knees and raised, tense shoulders.

**Safety:** Use a ball size you can handle comfortably. A no-ball walkthrough is fine. Do not force a stance width or ball height; stop if holding the position hurts.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (3 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

## Grip and Release Fundamentals

Key: `workout:grip-release-fundamentals` · Throwing Mechanics

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Explore a comfortable ball grip and a gentle release without squeezing or forcing your wrist.

**Setup:** Stand still in a clear space. Support the ball with both hands before placing your throwing hand on it.

**Equipment:** Comfortably sized football; ready partner for an optional gentle pass

1. **Find a comfortable grip.** Rest your throwing fingers around the ball where you can control it. Hand size changes which fingers reach the laces; no single finger count fits everyone.
2. **Check the pressure.** Hold it securely without crushing it. Your hand and forearm should not feel strained.
3. **Rehearse gently.** Without throwing, slowly rehearse bringing the hand forward and letting the fingers finish toward an imaginary target. Do not snap the wrist hard.
4. **Use a short pass if ready.** With a ready partner and clear area, try a gentle short pass. Return to a comfortable grip instead of chasing a perfect spiral.

**Coaching cue:** Control comes before distance. Let your coach help match the grip to your hand and ball size.

**Common errors:** Forcing all fingers onto the same lace positions as someone else. Squeezing hard or trying to create spin with a violent wrist snap.

**Safety:** Use only comfortable, low-effort movements. Never throw toward an unprepared person. Stop for hand, wrist, elbow or shoulder pain; tell a parent or guardian and seek qualified guidance.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (4 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

**Purpose:** Feel your lower body turn with a smooth throwing rehearsal instead of twisting only your arm.

## Weight Transfer

Key: `workout:weight-transfer` · Throwing Mechanics

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Move smoothly from a balanced setup toward the target without falling forward.

**Setup:** Stand in a comfortable throwing stance on level ground, with a safe target direction ahead.

**Equipment:** No equipment; ball optional

1. **Feel both feet.** Pause and notice that both feet support you. Avoid rocking far onto the back foot.
2. **Rehearse forward.** Make a slow throwing motion and let your body move gently toward the front side.
3. **Keep the step modest.** Use a comfortable step rather than reaching so far that your body has to chase the front foot.
4. **Hold the finish.** Finish without falling or needing a rescue step. Return and make the next movement smaller if needed.

**Coaching cue:** Transfer your weight with control. A long stride does not automatically make a better throw.

**Common errors:** Rocking far backward before every throw. Overstriding and collapsing over the front leg.

**Safety:** Start with an easy no-ball rehearsal. Do not lock or force the front knee. Stop for pain or loss of balance and ask for guidance before adding effort.

## Follow-Through

Key: `workout:follow-through` · Throwing Mechanics

- [ ] Coach Tae technique/content approval
- [ ] Age/experience adaptation and dose reviewed
- [ ] Safety reviewed

**Purpose:** Let a comfortable throwing motion finish naturally instead of stopping your arm abruptly.

**Setup:** Choose a safe target direction and begin in a comfortable set stance.

**Equipment:** No equipment; optional ball and ready partner

1. **Rehearse slowly.** Without a ball, make an easy throwing motion toward the target.
2. **Let the arm continue.** Allow the arm to slow naturally after the imagined release. Do not force it to hit your opposite pocket or any fixed finishing spot.
3. **Check your whole finish.** Stay balanced and let your back foot respond comfortably to the turn.
4. **Add a gentle pass if ready.** Only with a ready partner, use an easy short pass and notice the same smooth finish. Stop well before effort changes your comfort.

**Coaching cue:** The finish is the result of the motion. Do not yank your arm into a pose.

**Common errors:** Braking the arm suddenly just after release. Forcing the throwing hand to a fixed finish that feels awkward.

**Safety:** Never force the shoulder or elbow through a painful range. Pain is a reason to stop and tell a parent or guardian, not a cue to finish harder.

**Dose:** Coach sets your reps. Duration shown (3 min · draft estimate) is an educational draft, not a personal prescription.

**Setup:** Face a ready partner in a clear area at a comfortable short-pass distance, or choose an imaginary target for a no-ball version.

**Equipment:** Comfortably sized ball and ready partner; no-ball option available
